Once upon a time there was a little girl
called Anna who lived with her grandmother
in a small house near a great forest.
Every morning Anna gave water to the hens
and carried wood into the kitchen, and her
grandmother milked the white goat.
One day, however, the goat was not in the
garden. Her grandmother was very sad,
because the goat gave them milk every day.
"Where is my goat?" cried the old woman.
"She was here yesterday, but now the gate
is open." Anna looked at the ground and
saw the tracks of the goat on the path
to the forest. "Do not be afraid,
Grandmother," she said. "I will find your
goat and bring her home before night."
Then she put some bread and cheese in a
basket and called her dog, whose name was
Rufus. Together they walked into the
forest, where the trees were tall and old
and the air was cool even in summer.
The birds sang in the trees, and a small
stream ran beside the path. At first Anna
was happy, because the sun was shining and
the dog was running in front of her.
Rufus chased a rabbit, but he did not
catch it. After an hour, however, the path
became narrow and dark, and Anna could not
see the sky through the leaves.
Suddenly Rufus stopped and began to bark
at a large black bird that was sitting on
a branch. "Good morning," said the bird.
"Why are you walking alone in the forest,
little girl?" Anna told the bird that she
was looking for her grandmother's goat.
"I saw a white goat this morning," the
bird answered. "She was running towards
the river, and she was eating every flower
on the way." Anna thanked the bird and
walked quickly towards the river.
When she arrived at the bank, she saw
that the bridge was broken. The water was
deep and fast, and Anna could not swim.
There was no other bridge, and the next
village was far away. Meanwhile the sun
was moving across the sky. Anna sat down
on a stone and began to cry. She was tired
and hungry, and she did not know what she
should do. Then an old fisherman with a
long white beard came along the bank in a
small boat. "Why are you crying?" he asked
kindly. "My grandmother's goat is on the
other side of the river," said Anna, "and
the bridge is broken." The fisherman
smiled and said that he would carry her
across the river in his boat. Anna and
Rufus got into the boat, and the old man
rowed slowly across the water. While he
was rowing, Anna told him about her
grandmother and the little house near the
forest. On the other side there was a
green field with many flowers. Rufus
jumped out first and ran across the field,
barking happily. However, the goat was
not in the field. Anna and the dog
searched everywhere, under the bushes,
behind the rocks and in the tall grass,
but they could not find her. Anna called
the goat again and again, but nobody
answered. At last they heard a sad sound
from a high rock at the edge of the field.
The goat was standing on the top of the
rock, and she could not come down.
"How did you climb up there, foolish
goat?" Anna asked. The goat looked at her
and said nothing. Anna thought for a long
time. Then she took the bread and the
cheese out of her basket. She put the
cheese on a low stone near the rock and
stepped back. The goat was hungry, because
she had not eaten since the morning.
Slowly and carefully she came down from
the rock and began to eat the cheese.
While the goat was eating, Anna came
closer very quietly. Anna quickly tied a
rope around the goat's neck. "Now you will
come home with me," she said. They walked
back to the river, where the fisherman was
waiting for them in his boat. The old man
laughed when he saw the goat with the rope
around her neck. He carried them across
the water, and Anna gave him the rest of
her bread. Meanwhile it was becoming dark,
and the first stars appeared in the sky
above the forest. The forest was quiet,
and Anna was a little afraid. However,
Rufus walked in front of her and showed
her the way. They walked and walked
through the dark forest until the moon
rose above the trees. Sometimes they heard
the voice of an owl, and once a fox
crossed the path in front of them.
At last they saw a light in the window
of the little house. The grandmother was
standing at the door with a lamp in her
hand. When she saw Anna and the dog and
the goat, she ran towards them. "Anna!"
she cried. "You found her!" She embraced
the girl and gave the dog a big bone.
Then Anna told her grandmother the whole
story while they ate their supper.
That night the goat slept in the garden,
and the grandmother closed the gate
carefully. The next morning the
grandmother milked the goat and made
fresh cheese. "This cheese is for you,"
she said to Anna, "because you are a
brave girl." Anna laughed and gave a
little of it to Rufus. And from that day
the goat never ran away again.
